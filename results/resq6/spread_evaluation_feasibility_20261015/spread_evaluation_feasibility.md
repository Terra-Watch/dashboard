# RESQ6 Cell2Fire spread-evaluation feasibility

Audit scope: the four established development incidents only. Worktree revision `4650cc599d4476bdc29cedcc1d05b7ab3cf28334`. No held-out inputs were accessed, no simulator was run, and no data were acquired. This assesses Cell2Fire's conditional spread task separately from DMM onset prediction.

## Decision summary

- **Conditional spread simulation (A): demonstrable.** Cell2Fire takes a specified point ignition and fuel/weather/terrain inputs and has saved exact Boolean frames at 6, 12, 24, and 48 h for 60 members of each of four development cases. These are simulations, not observations. The synthetic cardinal-wind experiment previously completed in `results/resq6/wind_direction_cardinal_20261015_verified/` verifies the direction convention in a uniform synthetic grid; it does not establish predictive skill for real incidents.
- **Observed active-fire agreement (B): supplementary comparison only.** Event-associated VIIRS n/h detections and schedule are present. Current targets are cumulative nominal-detection cells, not a full fire footprint. Seven of eight official 24/48 h comparisons are 10.28–35.23 h early, the eighth is 0.63 h early, and there is no approved age tolerance. No per-cell valid-view/cloud mask exists, so non-detections are not trustworthy negatives. Historical agreement scores are reproducible but not contemporaneous perimeter validation.
- **Physical spread accuracy (C): not credibly verifiable with current local reference data.** No time-resolved incident fire perimeter or burned-area reference at 6–48 h was found. Local MCD64A1 is a monthly coarse burned-area product, with multi-day burn-date uncertainty in the derived summaries; it cannot validate these short horizons. No overlap/distance score should be presented as physical accuracy from current targets.
- **DMM onset and Cell2Fire spread are different tasks.** The supplied description identifies DMM as one-day-ahead onset forecasting; Cell2Fire is conditional on an ignition. No DMM-to-Cell2Fire interface was found in the inspected implementation. The referenced `main_core(2).pdf` was not present in the worktree or supplied attachment directory at the expected name, so no additional paper-specific label/metric claims are made.

## Current implementation trace

1. **Scenario identity and origin.** `scripts/resq6/run_dev.py` loads `data/resq5/protocol_setup.json` at module load. The four fixed coordinates, `t0_utc`, EPSG:32644 domain origin/dimensions, per-incident jitter seed, and weather-row window are reused. `t0` is a protocol origin (first event-associated detection minus 12 h, floored), not a measured physical ignition time. No DMM probability or predicted ignition is an input to this runner.
2. **Prescribed initial fire.** `run_member()` offsets the stored ignition by a seeded uniform-disk jitter (default radius 500 m), converts it to row/column at 30 m, and writes a single 1-based Cell2Fire `Ncell` entry to a temporary `Ignitions.csv`. Thus the initial condition is a prescribed point cell for each member, not an observed initial perimeter or a DMM onset probability map.
3. **Fuel and terrain.** `build_inputs()` samples the WorldCover raster at nine offsets per 30 m cell, uses the modal class, then `engine/fuel.py` maps classes to Cell2Fire fuel codes (including tree/mangrove→TL2 code 182 and grass/moss→GR1 code 101; other classes default/non-burnable per its table). It bilinearly samples the Copernicus DEM and derives slope from the elevation gradient. It writes `fuels.asc`, `elevation.asc`, `slope.asc`, and the Spain lookup table under a temporary shared directory. Aspect is not supplied.
4. **Weather and parameters.** Historical development runs use cached hourly ERA5-Land wind (`data/resq2/<id>/weather_era5land_native_wind.csv`), starting at `t0`. Every ensemble member gets one multiplicative speed factor U(0.7,1.3) and one additive direction offset N(0°,15°), uniform across its full series. `Weather.csv` uses `WS` in km/h (`m/s × 3.6`, floored at 0.1) and `WD` from the meteorological “from” bearing plus offset. Cell2Fire's reader conversion and row/column direction have already been tested by the separate synthetic cardinal experiment (2 deterministic repeats per cardinal case); do not repeat it here. Temperature/RH are present in local archives but the Cell2Fire `S` run does not consume them. `--ROS-Threshold 0.05` is used for the documented TL2 smouldering behavior.
5. **Simulator outputs and time.** The invocation in `run_member()` uses `--sim S --ignitions --nsims 1 --grids --final-grid --Fire-Period-Length 1.0 --weather rows --nweathers 1 --scenario 1 --seed 42 --ROS-Threshold 0.05`. It reads `ForestGrid6.csv`, `ForestGrid12.csv`, `ForestGrid24.csv`, and `ForestGrid48.csv` exactly; if any frame is missing, that member is an error. A value equal to 1 becomes burned/Boolean true. Raw Cell2Fire CSVs and generated ASC/Weather/Ignitions inputs live only in a `TemporaryDirectory` and are removed on return.
6. **Archived predictions.** The horizon-aligned artifact directory stores each successful member as compressed NPZ arrays `hour_06`, `hour_12`, `hour_24`, `hour_48`, plus member number, last frame and ignition row/column. Per-incident manifests record 60 members, grid shape, exact frame/source-frame hours, per-file SHA-256 and ignition row/column. The four incident JSONs report 60/60 members successful and exact horizons. Frames at 6/12 exist even though the VIIRS target is unavailable by those horizons.
7. **Observed grid construction.** `scripts/resq5/analyze.py:observed_arrays()` associates type-0 FIRMS detections to an incident component, retains confidence n/h for the current target, projects to EPSG:32644 and floors to global-origin 375 m keys. The cumulative detection keys are assigned to 30 m simulator cell centers using the same flooring. The label is a cumulative active-fire-detection mask; it is neither an initial fire state nor a mapped physical perimeter. All cells outside the positive mask are currently set to zero without a coverage mask.
8. **Scoring.** `run_dev.py:score()` and `rescore_fair.py:score()` calculate scikit-learn average precision (AP) and plain Brier MSE on all domain cells. The official protocol specifies AP and Brier, not ROC-AUC. The existing supplementary time/wind analysis script also computes ROC-AUC where both classes exist; that is a secondary diagnostic, not an official acceptance metric. `DECISION_RULE.md` calls a per-incident/horizon win/loss/tie from `AP_engine - max(AP_baseline_a, AP_baseline_b)` using the frozen ±0.05 margin; Brier does not affect the call. The latest audit records exact score reproduction from saved member grids: 0 wins, 1 tie, 7 losses. This task did not rescore.

### Provenance caveat in the saved run

`recovery_provenance.json` records the source paths and hashes, executable hash, lookup hash, input hashes, and environment (Python 3.10.13; NumPy 2.2.6; pandas 2.3.3; scikit-learn 1.7.2). It also records 60 members per development incident. However, the recorded historical hashes for `run_dev.py` and `rescore_fair.py` do not match the available source, as documented in the temporal-alignment audit. The archived per-member manifests lack `ignition_jitter_radius_m` and omit per-member wind perturbation parameters; inspection of an NPZ showed no wind-factor/direction-offset members either. Current `run_dev.py` contains fields for wind perturbations, but their absence from these archived artifacts prevents establishing the exact member-specific historical weather draws from the archives alone. Therefore the stored frames are renderable artifacts, but exact replay of the historical member runs is not fully established.

Event-linked n/h pass schedules are in the temporal audit. These are positive detections only; no valid-observation opportunity layer is present.

MCD64A1 HDF and derived burn-pixel CSVs exist for three cases (the fourth has HDF but no derived CSV). The local summaries identify approximately 463 m pixels and monthly burn-date outputs; uncertainties range 1–6 days where reported. One case, E2024-155286, has unresolved fetch-report size/listing provenance. This can be contextual burn-scar evidence, not a 6/12/24/48 h reference perimeter. No other incident perimeter, aerial mapping, ground survey, or temporally resolved burned-area source was found among the four development input folders. Per-cell cloud/clear-view/coverage masks were not found.

## Three distinct evaluation questions

### A. Conditional spread simulation

Yes, the code and saved simulation grids demonstrate how the specified point ignition propagates under the configured synthetic/model inputs. The simulation can be visualized at exact elapsed frames, but this is evidence that the model ran—not that it predicts observed fire growth. A scientific demonstration must state whether ignition is prescribed, list environmental assumptions/perturbations, show elapsed simulation time, and label the footprint “simulated.”

### B. Observed active-fire agreement

Potentially useful as a secondary diagnostic after deciding target timing and handling coverage. Existing targets are accumulated n/h detections. No observations exist by 6/12 h; 24/48 h have stale targets in most incident-horizon pairs; cells with no detection have no known valid observation opportunity. If shown, call it agreement with subsequent VIIRS active-fire detections, not ground-truth perimeter accuracy. Do not convert unknown cells to negative labels or imply the archived official 0/1/7 calls establish physical skill.

### C. Physical fire-spread accuracy

No. The current local data lack initial and horizon-specific measured perimeter/burned-area truth at the required temporal resolution. MCD64A1 monthly data cannot resolve an interval as short as 6–48 h; VIIRS thermal detections are not full perimeters. Minimum reference data are a georeferenced, time-stamped fire perimeter or burned-area/change map at a defined start and target time, valid-area/quality and uncertainty metadata, documented spatial resolution, incident association, and an origin convention. Temporal sampling/age must satisfy an owner-approved tolerance; no numeric cutoff is asserted here.
